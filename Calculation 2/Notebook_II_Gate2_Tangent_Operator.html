Horizontal Finite-\(\varepsilon\) Screening Jet and Deformation Intertwiner

Notebook II, Gate 2 - exact resonant source families, fixed-fibre PBW comparison of the moving targets, regulated opposite-polarization BCH conversion, periodic coefficient extraction, and the represented \(A_1\) connection equation.

GATE 2 CLOSED OUTCOME B AT A1 NOT AN ORDINARY SECOND SCREENING
Verdict. For each \(A_1\) root component and each integral monodromy label, the two regular parent screenings form exact intertwiners on horizontal resonant Fock families. Canonical PBW-flat source and target frames identify their moving Hom-spaces with one fixed leading Hom-space. After the scalar, \(z\)-independent anti-normal-ordering subtraction, the odd numerator is genuinely divisible by \(t=\varepsilon^2\), and its coefficient \(Q^{[1]}\) is finite, nonzero and linearly independent from \(Q^{[0]}\). The two target representations nevertheless follow different momentum curves. Their first represented-current coefficients do not agree, so \(Q^{[1]}\) obeys an inhomogeneous connection/deformation-intertwiner equation and is not an ordinary screening of the frozen Carrollian algebra in the PBW-flat frame. Gate 2 therefore closes with Outcome B at \(A_1\). Regulator and base-point changes are scalar triangular; general connection changes obey the full source-target gauge law. No quantum screening doublet and no nonlinear \(A_2/W_3\) theorem is claimed.

The Gate-1 local result remains true: the bounded density symbol \((\beta_1^{\rm sf}\varphi^-+B_{\rm diff}\varphi^+)E\) has remainder \((\beta_1^{\rm sf})^2/4\). Gate 2 does not erase that calculation. It shows why the local criterion was insufficient: the exact plane-to-cylinder one-form carries an affine logarithmic factor, and the barred parent normal ordering becomes anti-normal ordering. The parent-coefficient residue contains both effects and is not the residue of the truncated local symbol.

Definition 1.1 - exact regular branches

Put \(t=\varepsilon^2\), \(\alpha(t)=\alpha^++t\alpha^-\), and \(\bar\alpha(t)=\alpha^+-t\alpha^-\). Let \(b(t),\bar b(t)\in\mathbb C[[t]]\) be the unique regular solutions with the same nonzero constant term \(b_1=-2/\alpha^+\) of

\[ t b(t)^2-2\alpha(t)b(t)-4=0,\qquad t\bar b(t)^2-2\bar\alpha(t)\bar b(t)-4=0. \]

2. Common completed source and target category

Definition 2.1 - horizontal Fock families and fixed formal neighborhood

Let \(R=\mathbb C[[t]]\). For a formal momentum curve \(\lambda(t)=(\lambda^+(t),\lambda^-(t))\), let

\[ \mathcal F_R(\lambda(t))=\mathcal P_{\rm fin}\otimes R\lvert\lambda(t)\rangle, \qquad \widehat{\mathcal F}_R(\lambda(t))=\widehat{\mathcal P}_E\otimes R\lvert\lambda(t)\rangle, \quad \widehat{\mathcal P}_E=\prod_{E\ge0}\mathcal P[E]. \]

The zero modes \(p^\pm\) act diagonally on the vacuum and the negative oscillators give a PBW basis. The canonical PBW-flat trivialization

\[ \tau_{\lambda}:X\lvert\lambda(t)\rangle\longmapsto X\lvert\lambda(0)\rangle, \qquad X\in\mathcal P_{\rm fin}, \]

identifies each moving Fock family coefficientwise with its leading fibre. Requiring preservation of every negative-mode PBW monomial and of a chosen normalized vacuum vector fixes this trivialization uniquely. If only the vacuum line is retained and its normalization is relaxed, scalar frame freedom remains.

For displaying derivatives of sector translations, but not as the screening source, use the fixed-lattice formal neighborhood

\[ \mathscr N_{\Lambda_0}= \mathbb C[\Lambda_0]\widehat\otimes \mathbb C[[t,\eta^+,\eta^-]],\qquad p^\pm=\lambda_0^\pm+\eta^\pm,\qquad q^\mp=-\frac12\partial_{\eta^\pm}^{\mathfrak h}. \]

Thus \(e^{\gamma q^+}f(\eta^-)=f(\eta^--\kappa\gamma/2)\), and every moving translation factors exactly as

\[ e^{\gamma(t)q^+}=e^{\gamma(0)q^+} e^{(\gamma(t)-\gamma(0))q^+},\qquad e^{(\gamma(t)-\gamma(0))q^+}=1+O(t). \]

The first factor shifts the discrete leading lattice; the second is a \(t\)-adically small translation generated by the now explicit unexponentiated \(q^+\). This replaces the invalid moving direct-sum group algebra: distinct moving group-algebra basis vectors are never declared equal modulo \(t\).

Proposition 2.2 - common fixed-fibre Hom-space

Let \(\lambda_m(t)\) be the horizontal source curve of Proposition 3.2. Since \([p^-_{\rm sf},q^+_{\rm sf}]=[p^+_{\rm sf},q^-_{\rm sf}]=1/4\), the zero-mode exponential \(e^{b(q^++tq^-)}\) shifts \((p^+,p^-)\) by \((tb/4,b/4)\). Hence in the single-field normalization the two vertex shifts are

\[ \Delta_{\rm u}(t)=\frac14\big(t b(t),b(t)\big),\qquad \Delta_{\rm b}(t)=\frac14\big(-t\bar b(t),\bar b(t)\big), \qquad \Delta_{\rm u}(0)=\Delta_{\rm b}(0)=:\Delta_0. \]

Rootwise, the minus component is \(\mu(t)\kappa\alpha_i/2\) and the frozen dictionary is \(\mu=-b/2\). At \(A_1\), reducing with \(p_{\rm sf}=-\alpha_1\mathbin\cdot p/2\) and \((\alpha_1,\alpha_1)=2\) gives, consistently,

\[ \Delta p_{\rm sf}^-=-\frac12\alpha_1\mathbin\cdot \left(\frac{\mu}{2}\alpha_1\right) =-\frac{\mu}{2}=\frac b4. \]

The two target trivializations

\[ \tau_{{\rm u},m}:\widehat{\mathcal F}_R(\lambda_m+\Delta_{\rm u})\to \widehat{\mathcal F}(\lambda_m(0)+\Delta_0)[[t]], \] \[ \tau_{{\rm b},m}:\widehat{\mathcal F}_R(\lambda_m+\Delta_{\rm b})\to \widehat{\mathcal F}(\lambda_m(0)+\Delta_0)[[t]] \]

and the source trivialization \(\tau_{{\rm s},m}\) place both parent intertwiners in the same complete module

\[ \mathscr H_m={\rm Hom}_{\mathbb C} \big(\mathcal F(\lambda_m(0)),\widehat{\mathcal F}(\lambda_m(0)+\Delta_0)\big)[[t]]. \]

Creation exponentials are coefficientwise finite in energy, annihilation exponentials truncate on \(\mathcal P_{\rm fin}\), and the PBW maps are continuous. Hence the renormalized ordered vertices define continuous elements of \(\mathscr H_m\{z\}[\ell]\). The source of the screening theorem is the direct sum of the exact horizontal fibres over \(m\in\mathbb Z\); the ambient neighborhood \(\mathscr N_{\Lambda_0}\) is used only to write connection derivatives and to audit off-horizontal monodromy.

3. Complete oscillator and zero-mode embeddings

Proposition 3.1 - full Weyl embeddings \[ I_t(a_{A,n})=t^{-1/2}J^+_{A,n}+t^{1/2}J^-_{A,n},\quad I_t(\bar a_{A,n})=t^{-1/2}J^+_{A,-n}-t^{1/2}J^-_{A,-n}, \] \[ I_t(p)=t^{-1/2}p^++t^{1/2}p^-,\quad I_t(q)=t^{-1/2}q^++t^{1/2}q^-, \] \[ I_t(\bar p)=t^{-1/2}p^+-t^{1/2}p^-,\quad I_t(\bar q)=-t^{-1/2}q^++t^{1/2}q^-. \]

They preserve both parent Weyl algebras and make their images commute. Because the formulas contain \(t^{\pm1/2}\), these embeddings are formed after scalar extension from \(R=\mathbb C[[t]]\) to \(K=\mathbb C((t^{1/2}))\). The transported screening vertices and contracted currents used below lie in the regular \(t\)-adic subcategory. The plane zero modes are obtained by restoring the Feigin-Fuchs shifts \(a_0=\mathcal J_0+\alpha_{\rm pl}/2\) and \(\bar a_0=\bar{\mathcal J}_0+\bar\alpha_{\rm pl}/2\); these shifts are not absorbed into \(p^\pm\).

Proposition 3.2 - simultaneous monodromy section

For an integer Fourier label \(m\), define \(\lambda^\pm_m(t)\) by

\[ \lambda^+_m=\frac12\left(\frac{m-\delta}{b}+\frac{m-\bar\delta}{\bar b}\right),\qquad t\lambda^-_m=\frac12\left(\frac{m-\delta}{b}-\frac{m-\bar\delta}{\bar b}\right), \]

where \(\delta=t b^2/4\), \(\bar\delta=t\bar b^2/4\). Since \(b-\bar b=O(t)\), both \(\lambda^+_m\) and \(\lambda^-_m\) are regular series. They solve simultaneously

\[ \delta+b\lambda^+_m+t b\lambda^-_m=m,\qquad \bar\delta+\bar b\lambda^+_m-t\bar b\lambda^-_m=m. \]

Thus both parent one-forms have the same integral monodromy on one exact horizontal section of the momentum bundle.

Corollary 3.3 - no off-horizontal screening jets

If one perturbs the source by \(p^+=\lambda_m^++\xi\), \(p^-=\lambda_m^-+\nu\), the residual exponents are

\[ a_{\rm u}=b\xi+t b\nu,\qquad a_{\rm b}=\bar b\xi-t\bar b\nu. \]

For generic \(t,b,\bar b\), simultaneous resonance forces \(\xi=\nu=0\). Therefore arbitrary momentum jets are not objects of the finite-parent screening domain. This restriction is essential: the formal neighborhood of Definition 2.1 compares moving target sectors, whereas the source is the horizontal fibre itself.

5. Opposite-polarization BCH conversion

Theorem 5.1 - regulated conversion factor

Insert a radial damping \(0<\rho<1\) in each barred contraction. For one \(A_1\) field the annihilation and creation exponents \(A_\rho,B_\rho\) obey

\[ [A_\rho,B_\rho]=\frac{t\bar b(t)^2}{2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\rho^n}{n},\mathbf1 =-\frac{t\bar b(t)^2}{2}\log(1-\rho)\,\mathbf1. \]

Consequently

\[ e^{A_\rho}e^{B_\rho}=\bar Z_{t,\rho}\,e^{B_\rho}e^{A_\rho},\qquad \boxed{\bar Z_{t,\rho}=(1-\rho)^{-t\bar b(t)^2/2}},\qquad Z_t=1. \]

The factors are scalar, \(z\)-independent, and coefficientwise analytic in \(t\) at fixed \(\rho\). Define the minimal renormalized barred field by \(\widehat{\bar V}_t=\lim_{\rho\uparrow1}\bar Z_{t,\rho}^{-1}\mathbb{\bar V}^{\rm raw}_{t,\rho}\). A finite subtraction multiplies it by \(e^{c(t)}\), \(c(t)\in t\mathbb C[[t]]\). For a root vertex the exponent \(t\bar b^2/2\) is \(t\bar\mu^2(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)\).

6. Periodic logarithmic residue

Definition 6.1 - based logarithmic residue

Fix \(z_0\in\mathbb C^\times\) and the branch \(z=z_0e^{i\theta}\), \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\), \(\ell=\log(z/z_0)=i\theta\). Define continuously on logarithmic Laurent fields

\[ \mathop{\rm LRes}_{z_0}f:=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(z_0e^{i\theta})\,i z_0e^{i\theta}d\theta. \]

Equivalently, for \(r\in\mathbb Z\) and formal \(a\),

\[ \boxed{\mathop{\rm LRes}_{z_0}\big(z^{r-1}e^{a\ell}\big) =z_0^r\frac{e^{2\pi ia}-1}{2\pi i(r+a)},} \]

with the removable value at \(r+a=0\). It reduces to the usual residue at \(a=0\), but \(\mathop{\rm LRes}_{z_0}(d\ell)=1\). This is why a local total derivative cannot be discarded before the branch prescription.

Proposition 6.2 - horizontal periodicity and the boundary term

For a weight-one field with residual exponent \(a\), the based integral of the total derivative is

\[ \mathop{\rm LRes}_{z_0}\partial_z\big(z^{n+1}V(z)\big) =\frac{e^{2\pi i a}-1}{2\pi i}\,z_0^{n+1}V(z_0). \]

This off-horizontal logarithmic boundary anomaly is generically nonzero on the ambient neighborhood. On the exact source curves of Proposition 3.2, however, \(a_{\rm u}(t)=a_{\rm b}(t)=0\) identically as formal series, not merely at \(t=0\). Both boundary terms therefore vanish before coefficient extraction. The two finite-parent cylinder integrals are ordinary periodic screening modes on their horizontal fibres, and coefficient extraction in \(t\) commutes with the integral in the energy-product topology.

The logarithmic formula is consequently a derived representative of the primary horizontal parent-family prescription. Its separate logarithmic terms must be summed before applying the boundary test; differentiating the exact resonance equation cancels their first-order monodromy anomaly.

7. Coefficient operators

Definition 7.1 - renormalized parent charges and channels \[ \widehat Q_t=2\pi i\,{\rm LRes}_{z_0}\mathbb V_t,\qquad \widehat{\bar Q}_t=2\pi i\,{\rm LRes}_{z_0}\widehat{\bar V}_t, \]

These first act between their moving horizontal Fock families. Put them in the common fixed fibre by

\[ \mathscr Q_{\rm u}(t)=\tau_{{\rm u},m}\widehat Q_t\tau_{{\rm s},m}^{-1},\qquad \mathscr Q_{\rm b}(t)=\tau_{{\rm b},m}\widehat{\bar Q}_t\tau_{{\rm s},m}^{-1}. \] \[ Q^{(0)}(t)=\frac{\mathscr Q_{\rm u}(t)+\mathscr Q_{\rm b}(t)}{2},\qquad Q^{(1)}(t)=\frac{\mathscr Q_{\rm u}(t)-\mathscr Q_{\rm b}(t)}{2t}. \]

Set \(Q^{[0]}={\rm Coeff}_{t^0}Q^{(0)}(t)\) and \(Q^{[1]}={\rm Coeff}_{t^0}Q^{(1)}(t)\). The superscript in square brackets is a coefficient/flag label, not a mode number.

Theorem 7.2 - finiteness, source/target, and nonvanishing

Both trivialized charges belong to the same topologically free module \(\mathscr H_m\). Their constant terms are the same leading vertex map. Equality of the constant coefficients in this fixed PBW basis means, now legitimately, that \(\mathscr Q_{\rm u}-\mathscr Q_{\rm b}\in t\mathscr H_m\). Therefore \(Q^{[1]}\) is finite and continuous, with

\[ Q^{[1]}:\mathcal F(\lambda_m(0))\longrightarrow \widehat{\mathcal F}\left(\lambda_m(0)+\frac{\mu_1}{2}\kappa\alpha_i\right), \qquad \mu_1=-\frac12b_1. \]

In the canonical affine formal-neighborhood gauge, its density representative begins

\[ \boxed{\mathcal S_{\rm odd}^{[1]} =:\!\big(b_1\varphi^-+B_{\rm diff}\varphi^+\big)E\!:,} \]

where \(B_{\rm diff}=(b_3-\bar b_3)/2=-\alpha^-b_1/\alpha^+\). Both \(q^+\) and \(q^-\) in this representative are the explicit connection derivatives of Definition 2.1; they are not undeclared operators on a moving group algebra.

Nonvanishing does not rely on an off-resonant source jet. Take the horizontal vacuum with \(m=-2\). In the coefficient of \(z^{-1}\), the projection of \(Q^{[1]}\lvert\lambda_{-2}(0)\rangle\) onto the PBW vector \(J^-_{-1}\lvert\lambda_{-2}(0)+\Delta_0\rangle\) is proportional to \(b_1\), hence is nonzero. The corresponding projection of \(Q^{[0]}\) contains only the \(J^+_{-1}\) creation direction. Thus \(Q^{[1]}\ne0\) and the two coefficient maps are linearly independent.

8. The represented \(A_1\) coefficient equation

Proposition 8.1 - universal currents versus represented actions

Let \(T_{\rm p}(t)\), \(\bar T_{\rm p}(t)\) be the two parent Feigin-Fuchs tensors with symmetric parent normal ordering, transported with the embeddings above. Define

\[ M(t)=t\big(T_{\rm p}(t)+\bar T_{\rm p}(t)\big),\qquad L(t)=T_{\rm p}(t)-\bar T_{\rm p}(t). \]

Direct expansion of the universal fields, with the barred contribution converted before the limit, gives, up to commuting vacuum constants,

\[ M(t)=M^{[0]}+t^2M^{[2]},\qquad L(t)=L^{[0]}, \] \[ M^{[0]}=2J^+J^++2\alpha^+\partial J^+,\quad L^{[0]}=4\langle J^+J^-\rangle_{\rm av}+2\alpha^+\partial J^-+2\alpha^-\partial J^+. \]

Thus the universal noncentral current formulas have no explicit order-\(t\) coefficient. This statement does not set the first coefficient of the PBW-trivialized source and target representations to zero: their zero-mode eigenvalues move with their momentum curves.

Theorem 8.2 - PBW-flat deformation-intertwiner equations

On each horizontal source fibre, the own-factor weight-one commutator is the boundary term of Proposition 6.2 and vanishes because \(a_{\rm u}=a_{\rm b}=0\) identically. The cross-factor commutators vanish because the two embedded Weyl algebras commute. The scalar BCH subtraction does not change an intertwining equation. Hence, before taking any coefficient,

\[ [\widehat Q_t,L(t)]=[\widehat Q_t,M(t)]=[\widehat{\bar Q}_t,L(t)]=[\widehat{\bar Q}_t,M(t)]=0. \]

Write the PBW-trivialized source and target actions separately as \(U_{\rm S}(t)\), \(U_{{\rm T,u}}(t)\), and \(U_{{\rm T,b}}(t)\). Here \([Q,U]=U_{\rm T}Q-QU_{\rm S}\) is intertwiner notation. Applying the PBW maps preserves the exact equations but does not freeze the represented zero-mode eigenvalues. The two target paths are

\[ p^+_{{\rm T,u}}=\lambda_m^++\frac{tb}{4},\qquad p^+_{{\rm T,b}}=\lambda_m^+-\frac{t\bar b}{4}, \] \[ p^-_{{\rm T,u}}=\lambda_m^-+\frac{b}{4},\qquad p^-_{{\rm T,b}}=\lambda_m^-+\frac{\bar b}{4}. \]

Their relative first tangent is therefore

\[ \boxed{\Delta p^+=\frac{b_1}{2},\qquad \Delta p^-=\frac{B_{\rm diff}}{2}=\frac{b_3-\bar b_3}{4}.} \]

Using the mode formulas of [FH25, eqs. (2.15a)-(2.15b)], conjugating the leading currents through the PBW frames gives the target-action differences

\[ \boxed{\Delta M^{[1]}_{{\rm T},n} =\frac{b_1}{2}\bigl(4J_n^+-2\alpha^+\delta_{n,0}\bigr),} \] \[ \boxed{\Delta L^{[1]}_{{\rm T},n} =\frac{b_1}{2}\bigl(4J_n^--2\alpha^-\delta_{n,0}\bigr) +\frac{B_{\rm diff}}{2}\bigl(4J_n^+-2\alpha^+\delta_{n,0}\bigr).} \]

The common source first correction cancels when the two exact order-\(t\) intertwining equations are subtracted. Dividing by two yields, for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\),

\[ \boxed{ [Q^{[1]},U_n^{[0]}]_{\rm PBW} +\frac12\Delta U^{[1]}_{{\rm T},n}Q^{[0]}=0, \qquad U=L,M.} \]

This is the correct connection/deformation-intertwiner equation. It is inhomogeneous even though the universal fields have no explicit order-\(t\) term. For \(M_0\), with \(p^+_0=m/b_1\) and \(\alpha^+b_1=-2\),

\[ \Delta M^{[1]}_{{\rm T},0}=2(m+1),\qquad \boxed{[Q^{[1]},M_0]_{\rm PBW}=-(m+1)Q^{[0]}.} \]

On the \(m=-2\) component used in Theorem 7.2, this becomes \([Q^{[1]},M_0]_{\rm PBW}=Q^{[0]}\ne0\). Likewise \(\lambda_m^-(0)=\alpha^-m/(\alpha^+b_1)\), while the leading target minus momentum is \(p^-_{{\rm T},0}=\lambda_m^-(0)+b_1/4\). This gives \(\Delta L^{[1]}_{{\rm T},0}=b_1^2/2\), hence \([Q^{[1]},L_0]_{\rm PBW}=-b_1^2Q^{[0]}/4\ne0\). Therefore the PBW-flat coefficient is nonzero but is not an ordinary screening of the limiting algebra. This proves Outcome B rather than Outcome A.

9. Regularization, base point, and triangular flag

Proposition 9.1 - regulator, base point, and connection covariance

A finite barred subtraction \(e^{c_1t+O(t^2)}\) changes the odd coefficient by \(-c_1Q^{[0]}/2\). Changing the logarithmic branch or the base point on a fixed horizontal monodromy component changes the coefficient by a componentwise scalar multiple of \(Q^{[0]}\).

The PBW-flat connection is part of Definition 2.1. More generally, let the two branch frames change by \(g_{{\rm s},r}=1+tA_{{\rm s},r}+O(t^2)\) and \(g_{{\rm t},r}=1+tA_{{\rm t},r}+O(t^2)\), \(r={\rm u,b}\), and define their relative half-differences \(A_{\rm s}=(A_{{\rm s,u}}-A_{{\rm s,b}})/2\), \(A_{\rm t}=(A_{{\rm t,u}}-A_{{\rm t,b}})/2\). The odd coefficient obeys the exact gauge law

\[ Q^{[1]}\longmapsto Q^{[1]}+A_{\rm t}Q^{[0]}-Q^{[0]}A_{\rm s}. \]

A common frame change on both branches has zero relative half-difference and leaves \(Q^{[1]}\) unchanged. The chosen normalized vacuum vectors make the canonical PBW-flat frames unique. If branch-relative vacuum normalization is relaxed while every PBW monomial and only the vacuum lines are preserved, \(A_{\rm s},A_{\rm t}\) are scalar; together with finite subtraction and base-point changes this gives

\[ \boxed{Q^{[1]}\longmapsto Q^{[1]}+cQ^{[0]}} \]

A momentum-differential re-trivialization produces additional \(q^+\)- or \(q^-\)-type terms according to the displayed general gauge law. Simultaneously, \(\Delta U_{\rm T}^{[1]}\mapsto\Delta U_{\rm T}^{[1]}+2[A_{\rm t},U_{\rm T}^{[0]}]\) and \(\Delta U_{\rm S}^{[1]}\mapsto\Delta U_{\rm S}^{[1]}+2[A_{\rm s},U_{\rm S}^{[0]}]\). Thus the general equation \([Q^{[1]},U^{[0]}]+\Delta U_{\rm T}^{[1]}Q^{[0]}/2-Q^{[0]}\Delta U_{\rm S}^{[1]}/2=0\) is gauge covariant; the canonical construction has \(\Delta U_{\rm S}^{[1]}=0\). Such a re-trivialization changes the connection category and is not silently identified with a scalar scheme change or used to claim an ordinary screening on the original PBW-flat fibres. In the canonical affine formal-neighborhood gauge, \(B_{\rm diff}\) is fixed. This separates: (i) the parameter correction \(D\partial_{b_1}Q^{[0]}\); (ii) the BCH finite subtraction; (iii) scalar triangular flag mixing; and (iv) a genuine connection change.
