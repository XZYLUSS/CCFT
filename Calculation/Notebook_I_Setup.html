Audit protocol (P0)

The rule, stated so that the document actually satisfies it — this is a load-bearing displayed claim protocol, not an every-box protocol: every load-bearing displayed claim — anything the paper will cite or build on — is marked and appears in the Section 10 ledger. Purely definitional or expository boxes (conventions, definitions, intuition) may be unmarked; they introduce notation rather than assert results. Marks attach to boxes, not to individual lines; where a box mixes provenance, the surrounding text says which equation is which. The marks are:

  • derived — obtained here from verbatim input by a computation shown in full. Reproducible by the accompanying Python cell.

  • rewrite — a convention-preserving algebraic rewrite of source material (e.g. cylinder modes to plane OPE), or a field redefinition. The redefinition is always exhibited.
  • formulated here — formulated in this notebook; no prior statement was located in the dated search protocol of §1.1. This is a literature-search status, not a categorical claim of global novelty. Always accompanied by a full justification and an explicit statement of what would falsify it.

A load-bearing claim absent from the ledger is not licensed for the paper. Section 10 collects the ledger, including the zero-mode, ordering, contour and module assumptions that are not themselves displayed equations.

Layer discipline. The papers are the intended outputs: Paper 1, the screening theorem (this programme); Paper 2, the boundary Carrollian \(W_3\) algebra; Paper 3, the nonlocal screening algebra and its quantum group. Notebook IV may be merged into III only under the conditions recorded in ROADMAP.md.

Three recent results define the frontier. Fredenhagen and Hörl[FH25] construct the proper symmetric quantum Carrollian \(W_N\)-algebra by contracting two copies of a relativistic \(W_N\)-algebra at the level of the Miura operator, and prove that with an averaged ordering prescription the basic structure constants coincide with the classical ones. D'Arcy, Delfante and Fredenhagen[DDF26] derive, from asymptotically flat higher-spin Chern–Simons gravity in a flat diagonal gauge, a classical Carrollian Coulomb gas: celestial free bosons, Carrollian screening charges generating the residual pure-gauge transformations, and Carrollian Miura currents as the invariants. Fredenhagen, Prohazka and Tiefenbacher[FPT26] show that massless Carrollian free fields generically require nonregular states with a nonseparable zero-mode sector.

The gap is precise and is stated in the sources themselves. [FH25] lists as open both the role of screening charges in the Carrollian setting and the construction of representations from the free fields. [DDF26] constructs the screening charges only classically and presents quantisation as the next step. We have found no construction of the quantum screening operators of the symmetric Carrollian algebra as of 1 August 2026, on the basis of the following dated protocol: full-text bibliography searches for the identifiers 2509.14441 and 2604.27068; INSPIRE/publisher citation trackers on those two papers and on Fateev–Lukyanov; and keyword sweeps on Carrollian screening, Carrollian Coulomb gas, Carrollian Toda, quantum Carrollian W. The sweep is to be repeated at each drafting milestone and on submission day; a null result from a search engine is not a proof of absence, and unpublished work inside the originating group is not visible to it.

The object this programme builds

The quantum operators \(Q_i^{(0)},Q_i^{(1)}\) such that the currents of the proper symmetric quantum Carrollian \(W_3\)-algebra of [FH25] lie in their joint commutant,

\[ \mathcal W_3^{\rm Car}\;\subseteq\;\bigcap_{i,a}\ker\big(\operatorname{ad}Q^{(a)}_i\big), \]

and whose semiclassical limit is the set of four elementary bulk screening charges of [DDF26], eqs. (5.26a–d). Inclusion, not equality. Proving that the commutant is exactly \(\mathcal W_3^{\rm Car}\) requires a screening-kernel theorem — a graded character comparison, a cohomological (Felder-type) resolution, or a strong-generation argument — none of which is attempted in Paper 1. The semiclassical limit is the \(G\to0\) limit of the bulk normalisation D5, not an \(\hbar\to0\) limit: no \(\hbar\) appears anywhere in our oscillator algebra, and the earlier use of that phrase was meaningless.

2The parent relativistic \(W_N\)-algebra: conventions locked

The current and Miura conventions of this section are taken verbatim from [FH25], and we adopt their normalisations without modification even where another choice would be more familiar. §2.4 is the exception and is marked as such. [FH25] do introduce a potential — their §3.1 states \(J_j=i\,\partial\phi^{\rm FH}_j\) on the cylinder — so what §2.4 supplies is not a potential from nothing but an explicitly named notation bridge,

2.1Free fields and their brackets

Convention C1 — parent currents verbatim [FH25] (3.14), (3.4)

\(N\) spin-one currents \(J_i\), \(i=1,\dots,N\), with classical brackets and traceless constraint

\[ [J_{i,m},J_{j,n}]^{\rm cl}\;=\;m\,\delta_{m,-n}\Big(\delta_{ij}-\tfrac1N\Big), \qquad J_1+J_2+\cdots+J_N=0 . \]
Source note S1 — which equation this is

[FH25] states the free-field bracket twice: at (3.3), in setting up the classical Miura transformation, and again at (3.14), when preparing the contraction. We cite (3.14), and every calculation below uses it.

The reason for being explicit: equation (3.3) as printed omits the mode factor \(m\), while (3.14) contains it. This has been confirmed by direct inspection of the canonical arXiv v3 PDF (whose SHA-256 is recorded in the manifest). We record it as a source erratum note, not as a new result, and we do not speculate about its cause. It is immaterial either way: the \(m\)-carrying form is fixed independently and redundantly by (3.14), by the contracted brackets (3.15), and by the \(N=2\) normalisation (2.10), with which Check 2.1 shows it agrees. Anyone reproducing this notebook should read (3.14).

We abbreviate the pairing matrix as \(\kap_{ij}:=\delta_{ij}-\tfrac1N\), the projector onto the traceless subspace. Two distinct statements, kept apart. The displayed relation is [FH25]'s classical cylinder mode bracket (their \([\,\cdot,\cdot\,]^{\rm cl}=i\{\cdot,\cdot\}\)). Separately, we adopt the quantum radial plane OPE

\[ J_i(z)J_j(w)\sim\frac{\kap_{ij}}{(z-w)^{2}} \qquad\text{\small\color{#8d3b1e}{(adopted quantum convention, }} \text{\small\color{#8d3b1e}{not derived from the line above)}} \]

imported — it is [FH25]'s own quantum free-field normalisation (their §2, footnote 3, and §4.1), and it is the convention in which §§3 and 7.2 are computed. A classical cylinder Poisson bracket does not become a quantum radial OPE by a coordinate change, and an earlier draft wrongly labelled the passage a "rewrite". The two are used in different sections — OPE form in §§3, 7.2, mode form in §§4–6 — and their exact bridge is deferred (see the scope decision in §6 and Erratum E1).

Convention C2 — the \(N=2\) single-field normalisation verbatim [FH25] (2.10), fn. 3 \[ [J_m,J_n]=\tfrac{m}{2}\,\delta_{m,-n}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad J(z)J(w)\sim\frac{1/2}{(z-w)^{2}} . \]
Consistency check 2.1 derived

C1 and C2 must agree. For \(N=2\) the constraint gives \(J_1=-J_2\); writing \(J:=J_2\) we get \(\kap_{22}=1-\tfrac12=\tfrac12\), hence \([J_m,J_n]=m\kap_{22}\delta_{m,-n}=\tfrac m2\delta_{m,-n}\). Agreement is exact. This is the first of several checks that C1 and the \(N=2\) section of [FH25] are one convention, not two — a fact we will lean on heavily.

2.2The Miura transformation

Convention C3 — Miura operator verbatim [FH25] (3.1), (3.5), (3.6) \[ \prod_{i=1}^{N}\big(\alpha\partial-J_i\big)\;=\;(\alpha\partial)^{N}-\sum_{k=2}^{N}u_k\,(\alpha\partial)^{N-k}, \] \[ T=u_2=-\sum_{i1}(j-1)\,\partial J_j, \qquad c^{\rm cl}=-(N-1)N(N+1)\alpha^{2}. \]

The product is ordered with increasing \(i\) from left to right, and \(\partial\) acts on everything to its right.

Physical intuition — what the Miura map is doing

The Miura transformation is a statement about factorising a differential operator. The \(N\)-th order operator \(\mathcal L=(\alpha\partial)^N-\sum u_k(\alpha\partial)^{N-k}\) is the object whose kernel transforms covariantly; the currents \(u_k\) are its coefficients, i.e. its invariants. Writing \(\mathcal L\) as a product of first-order factors \((\alpha\partial-J_i)\) is a Miura/Darboux factorisation: the \(J_i\) are "square roots" carrying the gauge redundancy, and the \(u_k\) are what survives. In the Chern–Simons picture of [DDF26] this is literally a gauge choice — the diagonal gauge — with the \(J_i\) the diagonal entries and the \(u_k\) the gauge invariants. The screening charges will be the generators of the residual redundancy: they move the \(J_i\) and fix the \(u_k\). That is the entire logic of the Coulomb gas, and it is why "screening" and "pure gauge" are the same statement viewed from the two sides of the duality.[DDF26]

2.4Potentials, and the sign of the logarithm

Screening operators are exponentials of the potential, not of the current, so we must fix the potential's two-point function. This is the single most dangerous convention in the entire project: the sign here propagates into the screening condition, and a slip would flip \(F_0\).

Convention trap W1 — the logarithm sign is not free

Define \(\varphi_i\) by \(J_i=\partial\varphi_i\) and postulate \(\varphi_i(z)\varphi_j(w)\sim A_{ij}\log(z-w)\). Then, since \(\partial_w\log(z-w)=-(z-w)^{-1}\) and \(\partial_z\partial_w\log(z-w)=+(z-w)^{-2}\),

\[ J_i(z)J_j(w)=\partial_z\partial_w\big[\varphi_i(z)\varphi_j(w)\big]\sim \frac{A_{ij}}{(z-w)^{2}} . \]

Matching to C1, \(J_i(z)J_j(w)\sim\kap_{ij}(z-w)^{-2}\), forces

\[ \boxed{\;\varphi_i(z)\varphi_j(w)\sim +\,\kap_{ij}\log(z-w)\;} \qquad\text{\small\color{#8d3b1e}{(note the \(+\) sign)}} \]

The plus sign is a direct consequence of [FH25]'s choice \(J(z)J(w)\sim+\tfrac12(z-w)^{-2}\) (their footnote 3), which is itself opposite to the more common \(-\) convention of much of the Coulomb-gas literature. In the common convention one writes \(\varphi\varphi\sim-\log\) and inserts factors of \(i\) into vertex operators. This is a field redefinition, and it must be exhibited, not hidden. Let \(\phi_i\) be a canonically normalised real boson with the standard sign \(\phi_i(z)\phi_j(w)\sim-\kap_{ij}\log(z-w)\). Then

\[ \varphi_i:=i\,\phi_i \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \varphi_i\varphi_j\sim i^{2}\big(-\kap_{ij}\log\big)=+\kap_{ij}\log(z-w), \qquad J_i=\partial\varphi_i=i\,\partial\phi_i . \]

So [FH25]'s current normalisation is the statement \(J=i\partial\phi\) in terms of a real boson — consistent with the explicit factor of \(i\) in their cylinder mode expansions \(J_j(\theta)=i\sum_nJ_{j,n}e^{in\theta}\), \(\bar J_j(\theta)=i\sum_n\bar J_{j,n}e^{-in\theta}\) (3.11)–(3.12). Three consequences must be carried everywhere:

  • A vertex operator with real momentum in our convention, \(\no{e^{\beta\alpha_i\cdot\varphi}}\), is a vertex operator with imaginary momentum in the real-boson convention, \(\no{e^{i\beta\alpha_i\cdot\phi}}\). The factor of \(i\) has not disappeared; it has been absorbed into the field.
  • Hermiticity and reality conditions must therefore be stated in terms of \(\phi\), not \(\varphi\). \(\varphi\) is anti-Hermitian in the real form; \(\no{e^{\beta\varphi}}\) is unitary, not positive.
  • Every screening momentum, zero mode and cocycle below is transported through \(\varphi=i\phi\). Claims of "verbatim" adoption apply to [FH25]'s current normalisation \(J_iJ_j\sim\kap_{ij}(z-w)^{-2}\); the potential is introduced here, and the redefinition above is the bridge.

We nonetheless work with \(\varphi\) rather than \(\phi\), because it makes every current formula directly comparable with [FH25]. It does not make the vertex operators comparable with the standard Coulomb-gas literature without the translation above: in [FH25]'s own potential the screening momentum is imaginary. The price of the choice is exactly the bookkeeping listed above, and it must be paid explicitly in the paper's conventions section.

3The parent screening operators — the missing bridge

Why this section exists

[FH25] does not contain screening operators. The entire project consists of contracting objects that its authors never wrote down. Therefore the parent screenings must be imported from the Coulomb-gas literature[FL88],[BS93] and translated into [FH25]'s normalisation. Until that translation is done, symbols like \(b\) or \(\beta\) are meaningless and any statement of the form "\(F_0\) fixes \(\beta\)" is vacuous. 3.1The weight computation, done explicitly

We work first at \(N=2\) in the single-field language of C2/C4, where \(J=J_2\), \(\varphi=\varphi_2\), and by W1 with \(\kap_{22}=\tfrac12\),

\[ \varphi(z)\varphi(w)\sim\tfrac12\log(z-w), \qquad J(z)\varphi(w)\sim\frac{1/2}{z-w}. \]
Derivation 3.1 — conformal weight of \(V_\beta=\no{e^{\beta\varphi}}\) derived

With \(T=\no{JJ}+\alpha\partial J\) (C4), collect the three contributions to the singular part of \(T(z)V_\beta(w)\).

(i) Double contraction of \(\no{JJ}\). Each \(J\) contracts with the exponent, giving \(\big(\tfrac{\beta}{2}\big)^2(z-w)^{-2}V_\beta=\tfrac{\beta^2}{4}(z-w)^{-2}V_\beta\).

(ii) Single contraction of \(\no{JJ}\). Two ways, each giving \(\tfrac{\beta}{2}(z-w)^{-1}\no{J(z)V_\beta(w)}\); total \(\beta(z-w)^{-1}\no{JV_\beta}\). Since \(\partial_w V_\beta=\beta\no{JV_\beta}\), this is exactly \((z-w)^{-1}\partial_wV_\beta\) — the correct descendant term, which confirms the sign of W1. Had we chosen \(\varphi\varphi\sim-\tfrac12\log\), this term would have come out as \(-\partial V_\beta\), an immediate contradiction. The descendant term is the sign-checker.

(iii) Background charge. \(\alpha\,\partial_z J(z)V_\beta(w)\sim\alpha\beta\,\partial_z\big[\tfrac{1/2}{z-w}\big]V_\beta =-\tfrac{\alpha\beta}{2}(z-w)^{-2}V_\beta\).

Hence

\[ T(z)V_\beta(w)\sim\frac{h_\beta}{(z-w)^2}V_\beta(w)+\frac{\partial_wV_\beta(w)}{z-w}, \qquad \boxed{\,h_\beta=\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}-\frac{\alpha\beta}{2}\,} \]
Definition D2 — screening condition

\(Q=\oint dz\,V_\beta(z)\) commutes with all modes of \(T\) iff \(V_\beta\) has weight one, i.e. the singular part of \(T V_\beta\) is a total derivative. Therefore

\[ h_\beta=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta^{2}-2\alpha\beta-4=0 . \tag{$F^{\rm rel}$} \]

3.2The two branches, and where \(b+1/b\) lives

Derivation 3.2 — branch structure derived

The quadratic \(F^{\rm rel}\) has two roots \(\beta_\pm=\alpha\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2+4}\), obeying

\[ \beta_+\beta_-=-4,\qquad \beta_++\beta_-=2\alpha . \]

Parametrising \(\beta_+=2b\) and \(\beta_-=-2/b\) — which is consistent, since \((2b)(-2/b)=-4\) automatically — the sum relation gives

\[ \alpha=b-\frac1b, \qquad\text{and hence}\qquad c=1-6\alpha^{2}=1-6\Big(b-\frac1b\Big)^{2}, \]

which matches the standard Coulomb-gas central charge after the real-form continuation of W1 (see the qualification in the intuition box below); it is a convention match, not an identity with every Liouville normalisation.[DF84],[BS93] This is the validation of the whole normalisation dictionary: an independent, well-known relation is reproduced with no free parameters. Our conventions are the standard ones in disguise.

Notation reserved from here on. The symbol \(b\) denotes the Toda coupling and nothing else, related to the single-field screening momentum by \(\beta_+=2b\). The symbol \(\beta\) (with subscripts \(\beta_1,\beta_3\) after contraction) denotes the full single-field momentum, i.e. the root of \(F^{\rm rel}\). The two differ by a factor of two and must never be substituted for one another: \(\beta^2-2\alpha\beta-4=0\) but \(b^2-\alpha b-1=0\).

Physical intuition — what the two branches are

A Coulomb-gas boson with a background charge is a free field whose stress tensor has been "tilted" so that the vacuum carries charge. The tilt makes exactly two vertex operators marginal: the pair \((b,-1/b)\), dual under \(b\to-1/b\). Physically they are the two ways to add a unit of screening charge without disturbing conformal symmetry. The familiar Liouville statement — two exponential potentials with \(Q=b+b^{-1}\), the second invisible semiclassically — is the same structure in a different real form: our locked convention gives \(c=1-6(b-b^{-1})^2\) and \((\beta_+,\beta_-)=(2b,-2/b)\), with a relative minus sign and \(b-b^{-1}\) in place of \(b+b^{-1}\), reflecting the \(\varphi=i\phi\) continuation of W1. We therefore state this as a Coulomb-gas dual-screening pair rather than importing Liouville formulas, which would require the explicit continuation to be written out. The reciprocity \(\beta_+\beta_-=-4\) is the "\(b+1/b\)" structure. Remember this: in §7 we will see the Carrollian contraction keep one branch and blow up the other — and the blown-up branch does not vanish from the physics, it becomes the background charge.

3.3The \(A_{N-1}\) case and the root dictionary

Convention C5 — roots imported [FL88],[BS93]

The simple roots of \(A_{N-1}\) in the \(N\)-component basis are \(\alpha_i=e_i-e_{i+1}\), \(i=1,\dots,N-1\). Since \(\sum_a(\alpha_i)_a=0\), the trace-subtraction in \(\kap\) is invisible on roots:

\[ (\alpha_i,\alpha_j)_{\kap}:=\alpha_i^{\,a}\kap_{ab}\,\alpha_j^{\,b}=\alpha_i\cdot\alpha_j= \begin{cases} \;\;2 & i=j\\ -1 & |i-j|=1\\ \;\;0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases} \]

i.e. the \(A_{N-1}\) Cartan matrix. The parent screening currents are \(V_i=\no{e^{\mu\,\alpha_i\cdot\varphi}}\), with \(\mu\) the root-basis momentum.

Proposition 3.3 — general-root screening weight derived

In the locked normalisation, \(V_{\mu\alpha_i}\) has \(u_2\)-weight

\[ \boxed{\;h(\mu\alpha_i)=\mu^{2}+\alpha\mu\;}, \qquad\text{so the screening condition is}\qquad \mu^{2}+\alpha\mu-1=0 . \]

5.3The filtration lemma, proved before any OPE

The following is the structural fact that makes the whole project a finite computation. It is proved here abstractly, from the commutation relations alone — not verified case by case on OPEs, which would prove nothing general.

Lemma 5.1 (Filtration / Truncation) formulated here

Let \(\mathcal F^{r}\) denote the span of averaged-ordered products of the \(J^\pm_i\) and their derivatives containing at most \(r\) factors of \(J^-\) (counting fields, not parameters). Let

\[ \Scr^{(0)}=\no{e^{\beta\,\alpha_i\cdot\vp}},\qquad \Scr^{(1)}=\no{\big(A\,\alpha_i\cdot\vm+B\,\alpha_i\cdot\vp\big)e^{\beta\,\alpha_i\cdot\vp}} . \]

Define the degree map \(d(+)=0\), \(d(-)=1\). Then, with \(U^+_k\in\mathcal F^0\) and \(U^-_k\in\mathcal F^{\le1}\) (C12), the number of Wick pairings contributing to \(U^{\sigma}_k(z)\,\Scr^{(a)}(w)\) is bounded by \(d(\sigma)+a\). The bound counts pairings; it is not a bound on the pole order, nor on the number of terms:

\[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} & \Scr^{(0)} & \Scr^{(1)}\\\hline U^{+}_k & 0 & 1\\ U^{-}_k & 1 & 2 \end{array} \]

Proof. The only nonvanishing pairing is the crossed one (C7), so a contraction requires one \(J^-\)-type object and one \(J^+\)-type object. The exponent of \(\Scr^{(a)}\) contains only \(\vp\); the prefactor of \(\Scr^{(1)}\) contains at most one \(\vm\) and at most one \(\vp\). Hence:

  • \(U^+_k\Scr^{(0)}\): both sides are pure \(+\); \([\vp,\vp]=0\) gives zero contractions, so the OPE is regular.
  • \(U^-_k\Scr^{(0)}\): the unique \(J^-\) can pair with the exponent; nothing else can pair. One contraction.
  • \(U^+_k\Scr^{(1)}\): the \(J^+\)'s can pair only with the single \(\vm\) in the prefactor. One contraction.
  • \(U^-_k\Scr^{(1)}\): the unique \(J^-\) pairs with exponent or with the \(\vp\) prefactor (one contraction), and one \(J^+\) pairs with the \(\vm\) prefactor (a second). No third pairing exists.

Moreover, because \([J^-,\vp]\) is central, the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series terminates exactly:

\[ \big[J^-_{a,m},\,\no{e^{\beta\alpha_i\cdot\vp}}\big] =\beta\,\big[J^-_{a,m},\,\alpha_i\cdot\vp\big]\;\no{e^{\beta\alpha_i\cdot\vp}}, \]

with no higher terms, since \(\big[J^-,[J^-,\vp]\big]=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Lemma 6.2 (Scheme covariance) formulated here

The line \(\mathbb{C}\,\Scr^{(0)}\subset{\rm span}\{\Scr^{(0)},\Scr^{(1)}\}\) is independent of the ordering prescription; only the flag, not the individual operator \(\Scr^{(1)}\), is well defined. Consequently the doublet is canonically a two-step filtration, and any statement about \(\Scr^{(1)}\) must be invariant under \(\Scr^{(1)}\to\Scr^{(1)}+\lambda\Scr^{(0)}\).

Proof. The basic bracket \([\vm,\vp]\) is a c-number kernel. It does not follow that the commutator of a \(\vm\)-mode with a function of \(\vp\) is central — an earlier draft said so, and that sentence was false. What is true, and is all that is needed, is that the c-number bracket makes the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series terminate at first order:

\[ \big[\vm,\,e^{\beta\vp}\big]=\beta\,[\vm,\vp]\,e^{\beta\vp}, \]

i.e. a scalar kernel multiplying the same exponential — not a central operator, but an element of \(\mathbb{C}\,\Scr^{(0)}\). Proposition 7.2 — classification of the even jet derived

The sum channel also has an \(O(\eps^2)\) piece, \(Q_2:=\oint\tfrac{\beta_3+\bar\beta_3}{2}\,\vp e^{\beta_1\vp} =\tfrac{\beta_3+\bar\beta_3}{2}\,\partial_{\beta_1}Q_0\), and one might hope for a third screening. Reproducibility and offline verification rewrite

This interactive page fetches fonts, MathJax, Pyodide and SymPy from CDNs and therefore is not, by itself, an archival rendering. The package now also ships Notebook_I_Setup_Static.pdf, a static rendering that requires no network or browser runtime. What is mechanically certified is the source-and-verifier package and the byte identity of that static companion, not visual equivalence between the interactive and static renderings. The versioned, checkable objects are the HTML source, static PDF, offline verifier, mechanical freeze check, shared divisibility predicate, mutation regression, pinned environment and manifest. ("Frozen" is reserved for \(G_{0,\rm sci}\); see the gate statement below.) Seven companion components travel with the notebook:

  • Notebook_I_Setup_Static.pdf — a self-contained static rendering for archival reading. It contains no executable notebook runtime; the HTML source and offline Python verifier remain the computational artifacts of record.
  • verify_notebook_I.py — a standalone verifier, no browser and no network, exiting nonzero on any failure. Its scope is exactly: the embedded SymPy identity checks (classical Miura polynomials, root-weight algebra, oscillator brackets on the \(\delta\)-support, the \(N=2\) contraction, \(F_0/F_1\), the zero-mode half-shift, the projector identities) and the formal commuting-symbol jet checks. It does verify, within its adopted symbolic model, the \(N=3\) total-derivative witnesses (§10b) and the cylinder monodromy formula (§8). It does not verify source transcription, independent validation of the Wick/OPE implementation, averaged ordering, the filtration proof, cocycles, contour construction, operator domains, the even-jet commutant question, or the bulk dictionary. Those are prose or open.
  • A pinned environment for the authoritative check: Python \(\ge\) 3.10 with SymPy 1.14.0, enforced — the verifier now exits nonzero on a version mismatch (override with --allow-any-sympy for exploratory use only). The in-page runtime is not pinned: the browser loads whatever SymPy is bundled with Pyodide 0.26.4, which need not be 1.14.0. The embedded cells are therefore illustrative and convenient; the offline verifier is the artefact of record, and any discrepancy between them is resolved in favour of the verifier.
  • SOURCE_MANIFEST.sha256 — SHA-256 fingerprints. Actively hash-checked lines: all eight distributed artifacts — this HTML, the static PDF, verify_notebook_I.py, freeze_check.py, divisibility.py, test_freeze_gate_mutations.py, requirements.txt and ROADMAP.md. Not package-checked: the three primary-source PDFs. They are ordinary arXiv-generated PDFs with text layers; their SHA-256 digests have been independently recomputed by two separate parties and agree exactly with the values recorded in the manifest. They remain provenance comments rather than active entries for one reason only: third-party source files are not redistributed as part of this package. If a freeze bundle does include them, promote the three comment lines to active hash lines.
  • divisibility.py — the single divisibility predicate used by the offline gate components (the browser cell reimplements the test for convenience), with positive, negative and contract controls.
  • test_freeze_gate_mutations.py — a negative control for the gate itself. It breaks the shared predicate three ways: an always-true return, replacement of the cancelled quotient by the unevaluated quotient \(R/F\), and removal of the polynomiality rejection. It then stages the whole package and applies nine single-component bypasses: five in the verifier (including valid early-green and disabled-accumulator cases), two in the freeze checker, and two in the mutation runner. The manifest is regenerated after each mutation, so the test measures behavioural detection rather than a hash mismatch. Return code, a distinct completion sentinel and the reported PASS count must all agree. Every subprocess is bounded, and independent package mutations are evaluated concurrently in isolated temporary directories.
  • freeze_check.py — a behavioural freeze gate. Its predecessor searched for reassuring strings and was defeated by a mutation that kept the strings while disabling the tests; it now runs the controls, self-tests its own result accumulator, executes the verifier in ordinary and negative-control modes, validates their sentinels and check counts, validates the mutation runner's distinct sentinel and exact case count, and performs document-control scans (which are legitimately textual). A caller-side recursion guard prevents staged child gates from launching the mutation suite again.

sha256sum -c SOURCE_MANIFEST.sha256
python3 verify_notebook_I.py
python3 test_freeze_gate_mutations.py

must return zero. This is \(G_{0,\rm mech}\), the mechanical gate. It is not the scientific freeze:

\[ \begin{aligned} G_{0,\rm mech}&=\text{manifest}+\text{verifier}+\text{mutation regression}+\text{freeze checker},\\ G_{0,\rm sci}&=G_{0,\rm mech}+\text{human scientific sign-off}. \end{aligned} \]

The direct mutation-regression command and the freeze checker are deliberately cross-witnessed: the former mutates the latter, while the latter rejects a silent or sentinel-forging mutation runner. Only \(G_{0,\rm sci}\) authorises the word "frozen". The four commands returning zero establish \(G_{0,\rm mech}\) and nothing more. What the gate certifies is bounded: document control, regression against previously-found defects, the behavioural divisibility controls, and the successful execution of the verifier and mutation regression. The verifier certifies only the symbolic identities it lists — not source transcription, operator existence, averaged ordering, cocycles, contour signs, or the bulk normalisation map. A green gate is not a scientific freeze certificate for anything the ledger marks open. Cell numbering.
